\title[Simultaneous Borel saturation]{Simultaneous Borel saturation for chains
and antichains, and the prescription problem}

\subjclass[2020]{03E15, 06A07, 05C15, 05C69}

\keywords{Dilworth's theorem, Mirsky's theorem, Greene--Kleitman theorem,
saturated partition, Borel graph, Borel chromatic number, list colouring}

\begin{abstract}
We prove Borel simultaneous saturation theorems for chain partitions of
partial orders and, at finite height, for antichain partitions. The proof
passes through a graph theorem that combines finite simultaneous saturation
with exact Borel colouring. We also study prescribed blocks. Any finite
family of Borel chains whose prescription is consistent on every finite set
extends to a Borel chain partition with the same labels. More generally,
every feasible Borel finite-list assignment on the incomparability graph of
a Borel partial order has a Borel colouring. This result follows from the
countable chain cover and lexicographic representation of
Harrington--Marker--Shelah, together with compactness and analytic
separation. For two blocks, a parity argument applies to both chains and
antichains. In contrast, we give a height-three Borel order with three
prescribed finite antichains that have an abstract extension and satisfy
anchor saturation but have no Borel extension.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Dilworth's theorem identifies the width of a finite partial order with
the least number of chains needed to partition it~\cite{Dilworth1950}.
Mirsky's theorem gives the dual statement for height and antichains
\cite{Mirsky1971}. Greene and Kleitman strengthened these results by
considering unions of several antichains at once
\cite{GreeneKleitman1976}. If $P$ is a poset and $r\ge1$, put
\[
\alpha_r(P)=\sup\bigl\{|A_0\cup\cdots\cup A_{r-1}|:
 A_0,\ldots,A_{r-1}\text{ are disjoint antichains}\bigr\}.
\]
For a chain partition $\mathcal C$, the quantity
$\sum_{C\in\mathcal C}(|C|\wedge r)$ is at least $\alpha_r(P)$.
The finite Greene--Kleitman theorem produces a partition attaining
equality simultaneously for $r$ and $r+1$. Interchanging chains and
antichains gives the corresponding assertion for finite height.

For a Borel partial order, definability becomes a separate issue.
Harrington, Marker, and Shelah showed that a thin Borel order is covered
by countably many Borel chains, and gave a lexicographic representation
of such orders~\cite{HarringtonMarkerShelah1988}. The finite-width Borel
Dilworth theorem was established by Kada and subsequently strengthened
by Carroy, Miller, and Vidny\'anszky
\cite{Kada1989,CarroyMillerVidnyanszky2021}. We use these results to
ask for a Borel partition attaining two Greene--Kleitman bounds at the
same time. The answer is positive.

\begin{theoremA}
Let $P$ be a Borel partial order on a standard Borel space $X$ and
$r\ge1$. There is a Borel chain partition $\mathcal C$ of $X$ that is
simultaneously $r$- and $(r+1)$-saturated. If $P$ has finite height,
there is also a Borel antichain partition simultaneously saturated for
the dual norms.
\end{theoremA}

The common argument is a theorem about Borel graphs. It assumes finite
simultaneous saturation on induced finite graphs and exact Borel
colourability on Borel induced subgraphs. A finite set carries optimal
clique packings for both norms. Compactness fixes a partition of this
set compatible with every finite extension. The blocks that meet each
packing to capacity are then replaced by an exact Borel colouring.
For an incomparability graph these blocks are chains; for a
comparability graph they are antichains. The finite inputs are the
Greene--Kleitman theorem and its dual. The Borel inputs are respectively
Borel Dilworth and Borel Mirsky.

There is a further question about the labels of the blocks. Given
pairwise disjoint Borel independent sets $E_0,\ldots,E_{n-1}$ in a
Borel graph, we seek a Borel independent partition
$X=C_0\sqcup\cdots\sqcup C_{n-1}$ such that $E_i\subseteq C_i$.
The obvious necessary condition is that the same requirement can be
met on every finite subset of $X$. We call this the finite extension
property, $\mathrm{(FE)}_n$. Compactness shows that it is equivalent to
the existence of an abstract extension. Its Borel sufficiency depends
on the graph.

For two colours, the labels can be preserved in any Borel graph that
already has a Borel proper $2$-colouring.

\begin{theoremB}
Let $G$ be a Borel graph with a Borel proper $2$-colouring. Any
prescription by two Borel independent sets satisfying
$\mathrm{(FE)}_2$ extends to a Borel proper $2$-colouring.
\end{theoremB}

Indeed, the prescribed labels determine a flip of a background
colouring on the components that meet the prescription. Path parity
makes the flip consistent, and invariant analytic separation makes it
Borel. Thus Theorem B applies to both chains of width-two Borel orders
and antichains of height-two Borel orders.

For chains, finite extension suffices for every finite number of
labels. The prescribed sets need not be finite.

\begin{theoremC}
Let $P=(X,\le_P)$ be a Borel partial order and $n\ge1$. Suppose
$E_0,\ldots,E_{n-1}$ are pairwise disjoint Borel subsets of $X$ and
every finite $F\subseteq X$ can be partitioned into chains
$F=D_0\sqcup\cdots\sqcup D_{n-1}$ with $F\cap E_i\subseteq D_i$.
Then there is a Borel chain partition
$X=C_0\sqcup\cdots\sqcup C_{n-1}$ with $E_i\subseteq C_i$ for every
$i<n$.
\end{theoremC}

We prove a stronger finite-list theorem. At each point, a Borel list
specifies the colours still available; finite feasibility is the only
hypothesis. The proof keeps one joint abstract colouring possible while
shrinking all lists. The countable Borel chain cover of
Harrington--Marker--Shelah supplies a background chain label. Compact
sets record where target colours may occur along these chains.
Incomparability across distinct real positions then occurs only at
countably many endpoints of gaps. Analytic separation turns the
restrictions into Borel lists without losing joint feasibility. The
lexicographic representation reduces the remaining problem to
countable Borel fibres, where compactness yields a Borel choice. This
proof also gives finite Borel Dilworth as the case in which every
initial list is the full palette; the chain extension theorem does
not use finite Borel Dilworth as an input.

For general Borel graphs, we give a separate sufficient condition in
terms of anchored decomposition and a Borel puncturing property.
These conditions support a stepwise extension that meets every
maximum clique at each step. The distinction matters on the
antichain side: finite extension alone fails at height three, even
with saturated finite anchors.

\begin{theoremD}
There are a Borel partial order of height three and three prescribed
three-element Borel antichains satisfying $\mathrm{(FE)}_3$ and anchor
saturation that admit an abstract partition into three antichains
but no such Borel partition.
\end{theoremD}

The example encodes a Borel graph of ordinary chromatic number two
and Borel chromatic number greater than two. Any Borel extension of
the prescribed antichains would induce a Borel $2$-colouring of that
graph. Thus Theorem C has no antichain analogue under the same finite
hypothesis.

We use the graph formulation for the saturation and two-colour
results. We then treat the general conditional extension criterion,
prove the chain theorem, and finish with the height-three example.
The chain theorem also permits the finite optimal template in the
saturation argument to be preserved block by block.

\subsection*{Notation}
All spaces are standard Borel. A graph on $X$ is a symmetric,
irreflexive subset of $X^2$, and $G\restr Y=G\cap(Y\times Y)$.
For a partial order $P$ we write $x\perp_Py$ when $x\ne y$ are
incomparable. We identify each natural number $n$ with
$\{0,\ldots,n-1\}$. Partitions indexed by $n$ may have empty
parts; unindexed partitions have no empty parts. Addition modulo
$2$ is denoted by $\oplus$, and $\mathbf1_H$ is the characteristic
function of $H$.

\section{Cliques, independent sets, and saturation}
\label{sec:prelim}

Let $G$ be a graph on $X$. A set $I\subseteq X$ is \emph{independent} if no two
of its points are $G$-adjacent, and $K\subseteq X$ is a \emph{clique} if any two
distinct points of $K$ are. We write
\[
\cl(Y)=\sup\{|K|:K\subseteq Y\ \text{a clique}\}
\]
for the clique number of $G\restr Y$, and for $t\ge1$
\[
\cl_t(Y)=\sup\bigl\{|K_0\cup\dots\cup K_{t-1}|:
K_0,\dots,K_{t-1}\subseteq Y\ \text{pairwise disjoint cliques}\bigr\}.
\]
Thus $\cl_1=\cl$, and $\cl_t(Y)\le t\,\cl(Y)$, so that all the $\cl_t(Y)$ are
finite as soon as $\cl(Y)$ is, in which case the suprema are attained. An
\emph{independent partition} of $Y$ is a partition of $Y$ all of whose blocks
are independent, and its \emph{$t$-norm} is
\[
\lVert\mathcal P\rVert_t=\sum_{I\in\mathcal P}(|I|\wedge t),
\qquad |I|\wedge t=\min(|I|,t),
\]
with $|I|\wedge t=t$ for infinite $I$. We call $\mathcal P$ \emph{$t$-saturated}
if $\lVert\mathcal P\rVert_t=\cl_t(Y)$, and \emph{simultaneously
$(r,r{+}1)$-saturated} if it is $r$- and $(r{+}1)$-saturated.

\begin{lemma}[Weak duality]
\label{lem:weak-duality}
For every $Y\subseteq X$, every independent partition $\mathcal P$ of $Y$ and
every $t\ge1$ one has $\cl_t(Y)\le\lVert\mathcal P\rVert_t$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $K_0,\dots,K_{t-1}\subseteq Y$ be pairwise disjoint cliques and
$T=K_0\cup\dots\cup K_{t-1}$. An independent set meets a clique in at most one
point, so $|I\cap T|\le t$ for every $I\in\mathcal P$; as also
$|I\cap T|\le|I|$, we get $|I\cap T|\le|I|\wedge t$ and hence
$|T|=\sum_{I}|I\cap T|\le\lVert\mathcal P\rVert_t$. Take the supremum over
packings.
\end{proof}

Fix now an algebra $\mathcal A$ of subsets of $X$ containing the singletons; in
all applications $\mathcal A$ is either the $\sigma$-algebra of Borel sets or the
power set of $X$. Two hypotheses on $(G,\mathcal A)$ will be used.

\begin{itemize}
\item[$\mathrm{(FS)}$] \emph{Finite saturation.} For every finite
$F\subseteq X$ and every $r\ge1$ the graph $G\restr F$ has a simultaneously
$(r,r{+}1)$-saturated independent partition.
\item[$\mathrm{(EC)}$] \emph{Exact colouring.} For every $Y\in\mathcal A$ the
graph $G\restr Y$ has an independent partition into $\cl(Y)$ blocks, all
belonging to $\mathcal A$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Examples}

Let $P=(X,\le_P)$ be a partial order, and let
\[
G^{\perp}=\{(x,y):x\perp_Py\},
\qquad
G^{<}=\{(x,y):x<_Py\ \text{or}\ y<_Px\}
\]
be its incomparability and comparability graphs. Independent sets of
$G^{\perp}$ are the chains of $P$ and its cliques the antichains, so
$\cl(G^{\perp})=\wdt(P)$, $\cl_t=\alpha_t$, independent partitions are chain
partitions and $\lVert\cdot\rVert_t$ is the Greene--Kleitman norm. Dually,
independent sets of $G^{<}$ are the antichains and its cliques the chains,
$\cl(G^{<})=\hgt(P)$, $\cl_t=\beta_t$, and $\lVert\cdot\rVert_t$ is the dual
norm. Under this dictionary, $\mathrm{(FS)}$ for $G^{\perp}$ is the
Greene--Kleitman theorem~\cite{GreeneKleitman1976} applied to the finite induced
subposets, and for $G^{<}$ its antichain dual
\cite[Theorem~3.1]{Greene1976}; $\mathrm{(EC)}$ with
$\mathcal A=\mathcal P(X)$ is Dilworth's, resp.\ Mirsky's, theorem for orders of
finite width, resp.\ height, and with $\mathcal A$ Borel it is the Borel
Dilworth, resp.\ Borel Mirsky, theorem~\cite{Kada1989,
CarroyMillerVidnyanszky2021}. In the Borel cases $\mathrm{(EC)}$ holds for
\emph{every} Borel $Y$, since $P\restr Y$ is again a Borel partial order on a
standard Borel space of no larger width, resp.\ height.

\section{Simultaneous saturation}
\label{sec:saturation}

Throughout this section $G$ is a graph on $X$ with $\cl(X)<\infty$ satisfying
$\mathrm{(FS)}$, and $r\ge1$ is fixed. Write $a_r=\cl_r(X)$ and
$a_{r+1}=\cl_{r+1}(X)$, both finite, and fix pairwise disjoint cliques
$K_0,\dots,K_{r-1}$ and $L_0,\dots,L_r$ with
\[
U=K_0\cup\dots\cup K_{r-1},\quad |U|=a_r,
\qquad
V=L_0\cup\dots\cup L_r,\quad |V|=a_{r+1},
\]
and set $S=U\cup V$, a finite set. Since the two packings lie in every
$Z\supseteq S$ while every $Z\subseteq X$ obeys the global bounds,
\begin{equation}
\cl_r(Z)=a_r
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\cl_{r+1}(Z)=a_{r+1}
\qquad (S\subseteq Z\subseteq X).
\label{eq:norms-stable}
\end{equation}
For a partition $\mathcal D$ of $Z\supseteq S$ put
$\mathcal D\restr S=\{D\cap S:D\in\mathcal D,\ D\cap S\neq\varnothing\}$; we say
$\mathcal D$ \emph{extends} $\mathcal E$ if $\mathcal D\restr S=\mathcal E$.

The following is the device that transfers finite information to the whole
space. Its proof is a finite intersection argument, made possible by the
finiteness of $S$.

\begin{lemma}[Locked template]
\label{lem:locked}
There is an independent partition $\mathcal E$ of $S$ such that for every
finite $F\subseteq X\setminus S$ some independent partition of $S\cup F$
extends $\mathcal E$ and has $r$-norm $a_r$ and $(r{+}1)$-norm $a_{r+1}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\Pi$ be the set of independent partitions of $S$, a finite set, and for
finite $F\subseteq X\setminus S$ let $\Pi_F$ consist of those
$\mathcal E\in\Pi$ admitting such an extension over $S\cup F$.

Each $\Pi_F$ is nonempty. By $\mathrm{(FS)}$ the finite graph
$G\restr(S\cup F)$ has a simultaneously $(r,r{+}1)$-saturated independent
partition $\mathcal D$, whose norms are $a_r$ and $a_{r+1}$ by
\eqref{eq:norms-stable}; then $\mathcal D\restr S\in\Pi_F$.

The family is monotone: $F\subseteq F'$ implies $\Pi_{F'}\subseteq\Pi_F$.
Indeed, if $\mathcal D'$ witnesses $\mathcal E\in\Pi_{F'}$, then the
restriction $\mathcal D$ of $\mathcal D'$ to $S\cup F$ is an independent
partition extending $\mathcal E$, and from
$|D'\cap(S\cup F)|\wedge t\le|D'|\wedge t$ we get
$\lVert\mathcal D\rVert_t\le\lVert\mathcal D'\rVert_t$ for $t\in\{r,r+1\}$,
while Lemma~\ref{lem:weak-duality} and \eqref{eq:norms-stable} give the reverse
inequalities; so both norms are again $a_r$ and $a_{r+1}$.

Finally $\bigcap_F\Pi_F\neq\varnothing$. Otherwise choose for each
$\mathcal E\in\Pi$ a finite $F_{\mathcal E}\subseteq X\setminus S$ with
$\mathcal E\notin\Pi_{F_{\mathcal E}}$; then
$F^{*}=\bigcup_{\mathcal E\in\Pi}F_{\mathcal E}$ is finite, and any
$\mathcal E^{*}\in\Pi_{F^{*}}$ satisfies
$\mathcal E^{*}\in\Pi_{F_{\mathcal E^{*}}}$ by monotonicity, a contradiction.
Any member of the intersection is as required.
\end{proof}

Fix such an $\mathcal E$ for the remainder of the section. Taking
$F=\varnothing$ gives
\begin{equation}
\lVert\mathcal E\rVert_r=a_r,
\qquad
\lVert\mathcal E\rVert_{r+1}=a_{r+1}.
\label{eq:template-norms}
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}
\label{def:active}
A block $E\in\mathcal E$ is \emph{active} if $|E\cap U|=r$ and
$|E\cap V|=r+1$. We list the active blocks as $E_0,\dots,E_{\ell-1}$ and put
$E_I=\bigcup_{i\in I}E_i$ for $I\subseteq\ell$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:active-points}
For all $i<\ell$, $j<r$ and $j'\le r$ the sets $E_i\cap K_j$ and
$E_i\cap L_{j'}$ are singletons, say $\{e_i^{\,j}\}$ and $\{f_i^{\,j'}\}$.
Consequently $|E_i|\wedge r=r$ and $|E_i|\wedge(r+1)=r+1$, and for each $j<r$
and $j'\le r$ the sets
\[
\widetilde K_j=\{e_p^{\,j}:p<\ell\},
\qquad
\widetilde L_{j'}=\{f_p^{\,j'}:p<\ell\}
\]
are cliques of cardinality $\ell$ meeting every active block in exactly one
point.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$E_i$ is independent and $K_j$ is a clique, so $|E_i\cap K_j|\le1$; since
$U=\bigsqcup_{j<r}K_j$ and $|E_i\cap U|=r$, all $r$ intersections are
singletons, and likewise for $V=\bigsqcup_{j'\le r}L_{j'}$. The sets
$\widetilde K_j$ have $\ell$ elements because the active blocks are pairwise
disjoint, and are cliques as subsets of $K_j$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:active-decomposition}
For every finite $F\subseteq X\setminus S$ there is a partition
$F=F_0\sqcup\dots\sqcup F_{\ell-1}$ with $E_i\cup F_i$ independent for each
$i<\ell$. In particular $\ell=0$ forces $X=S$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mathcal D$ extend $\mathcal E$ over $S\cup F$ with norms $a_r$ and
$a_{r+1}$. Every $D\in\mathcal D$ is independent and $U$ is a union of $r$
disjoint cliques, so $|D\cap U|\le|D|\wedge r$ as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:weak-duality}; summing over $\mathcal D$,
\[
a_r=|U|=\sum_{D\in\mathcal D}|D\cap U|
\le\sum_{D\in\mathcal D}(|D|\wedge r)=\lVert\mathcal D\rVert_r=a_r,
\]
whence $|D\cap U|=|D|\wedge r$ for every $D$, and likewise
$|D\cap V|=|D|\wedge(r+1)$.

Let $D\in\mathcal D$ meet $F$. If $|D\cap U|<r$ then $|D|\wedge r<r$, so
$|D|\wedge r=|D|$ and $D=D\cap U\subseteq S$, contradicting
$D\cap F\neq\varnothing$; hence $|D\cap U|=r$, and $|D\cap V|=r+1$ for the same
reason. As $U,V\subseteq S$ and $\mathcal D$ extends $\mathcal E$, the trace
$D\cap S$ is an active block.

For $i<\ell$ let $D_i$ be the unique block of $\mathcal D$ with
$D_i\cap S=E_i$, and set $F_i=D_i\cap F$. By the previous paragraph the $D_i$
cover $F$, so $F=F_0\sqcup\dots\sqcup F_{\ell-1}$, and
$E_i\cup F_i\subseteq D_i$ is independent. If $\ell=0$ then every finite
$F\subseteq X\setminus S$ is empty, i.e.\ $X=S$.
\end{proof}

From now on put $Y=(X\setminus S)\cup E_{\ell}$, so that
$Y\cap S=E_{\ell}$.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:clique-number}
$\cl(Y)=\ell$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A clique of size $\ell+1$ contains a finite one, so it suffices to bound
finite cliques. Let $K\subseteq Y$ be a finite clique and $F=K\setminus S$;
decomposing $F=F_0\sqcup\dots\sqcup F_{\ell-1}$ as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:active-decomposition} and using $Y\cap S=E_{\ell}$ we get
$K\subseteq\bigcup_{i<\ell}(E_i\cup F_i)$, and each $E_i\cup F_i$ is
independent, hence meets $K$ at most once; so $|K|\le\ell$. Conversely, if
$\ell>0$ then $\widetilde K_0\subseteq E_{\ell}\subseteq Y$ is a clique of
size $\ell$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:active-points}, while if $\ell=0$ then $Y$ is
empty by Lemma~\ref{lem:active-decomposition}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:main-saturation}
Let $X$ be a set, $\mathcal A$ an algebra of subsets of $X$ containing the
singletons, and $G$ a graph on $X$ with $\cl(X)<\infty$ satisfying
$\mathrm{(FS)}$ and $\mathrm{(EC)}$. Then for every $r\ge1$ there is a finite
independent partition $\mathcal C$ of $X$, with all blocks in $\mathcal A$,
such that
\[
\lVert\mathcal C\rVert_r=\cl_r(X)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\lVert\mathcal C\rVert_{r+1}=\cl_{r+1}(X).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $r$ and keep the notation above. If $\ell=0$ then $X=S$ and
$\mathcal C=\mathcal E$ will do, its blocks being finite and
\eqref{eq:template-norms} being the conclusion.

Assume $\ell>0$. Since $S$ is finite and $\mathcal A$ is an algebra containing
the singletons, $Y\in\mathcal A$; by Lemma~\ref{lem:clique-number}
$\cl(Y)=\ell$, so $\mathrm{(EC)}$ provides an independent partition
$Y=C_0\sqcup\dots\sqcup C_{\ell-1}$ with all $C_i\in\mathcal A$.

Fix $j<r$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:active-points} the set $\widetilde K_j\subseteq Y$
is a clique with $\ell$ elements, and each of the $\ell$ independent sets
$C_i$ meets it at most once, hence exactly once. As the $K_j$ $(j<r)$ are
pairwise disjoint, this exhibits $r$ distinct points in each $C_i$, so
$|C_i|\wedge r=r$; running the argument with $\widetilde L_{j'}$ $(j'\le r)$
gives $|C_i|\wedge(r+1)=r+1$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:active-points} the active
blocks satisfy the same two equalities, so $C_0,\dots,C_{\ell-1}$ contribute to
the two norms exactly what $E_0,\dots,E_{\ell-1}$ contribute. Since
$X=Y\sqcup(S\setminus E_{\ell})$ and the inactive blocks of $\mathcal E$
partition $S\setminus E_{\ell}$ into finite independent sets,
\[
\mathcal C=\{C_i:i<\ell\}\cup\bigl(\mathcal E\setminus\{E_i:i<\ell\}\bigr)
\]
is a finite independent partition of $X$ with blocks in $\mathcal A$, and by
\eqref{eq:template-norms}
\[
\lVert\mathcal C\rVert_r=\lVert\mathcal E\rVert_r=a_r,
\qquad
\lVert\mathcal C\rVert_{r+1}=\lVert\mathcal E\rVert_{r+1}=a_{r+1}.
\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:borel-chain}
Let $P$ be a Borel partial order of finite width on a standard Borel space $X$
and let $r\ge1$. Then $X$ has a finite Borel chain partition $\mathcal C$ with
$\lVert\mathcal C\rVert_r=\alpha_r(X)$ and
$\lVert\mathcal C\rVert_{r+1}=\alpha_{r+1}(X)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:main-saturation} to $G^{\perp}$ with $\mathcal A$ the
Borel sets; $\cl(X)=\wdt(P)<\infty$, $\mathrm{(FS)}$ is the Greene--Kleitman
theorem and $\mathrm{(EC)}$ the Borel Dilworth theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:borel-antichain}
Let $P$ be a Borel partial order of finite height on a standard Borel space $X$
and let $r\ge1$. Then $X$ has a finite Borel antichain partition
$\mathcal A$ with $\lVert\mathcal A\rVert_r=\beta_r(X)$ and
$\lVert\mathcal A\rVert_{r+1}=\beta_{r+1}(X)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
As above with $G^{<}$, using the antichain dual of the Greene--Kleitman
theorem and the Borel Mirsky theorem.
\end{proof}

It remains to remove the finiteness assumption on the width, which is a matter
of disposing of two degenerate cases.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:infinite-width}
Let $P$ be a Borel partial order of infinite width on a standard Borel space
$X$, and let $t\ge1$. Then $X$ has a Borel chain partition $\mathcal C$ with
$\lVert\mathcal C\rVert_t=\alpha_t(X)$; the same $\mathcal C$ works for all
$t$ simultaneously.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By the Harrington--Marker--Shelah dichotomy~\cite{HarringtonMarkerShelah1988},
either $P$ contains a perfect antichain or $X$ is covered by countably many
Borel chains.

In the first case $X$ is uncountable, so $|X|=2^{\aleph_0}$, and a perfect
antichain has cardinality $2^{\aleph_0}$, whence
$\alpha_t(X)=2^{\aleph_0}=|X|$ for every $t$. The partition into singletons is
Borel and has $t$-norm $|X|$.

In the second case, refining a countable Borel chain cover to a partition
gives a Borel chain partition $\mathcal C=\{C_n:n<N\}$ with $N\le\omega$ and
all $C_n$ nonempty. If $N$ were finite, $\wdt(P)\le N$ would be finite; so
$N=\omega$ and $\lVert\mathcal C\rVert_t=\sum_{n<\omega}(|C_n|\wedge
t)=\aleph_0$ for every $t$. On the other hand every antichain is countable, so
$\alpha_t(X)\le\aleph_0$, while $\wdt(P)$ being infinite there are antichains
of every finite size, so $\alpha_t(X)\ge\alpha_1(X)=\aleph_0$. Hence
$\alpha_t(X)=\aleph_0=\lVert\mathcal C\rVert_t$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem A]
The chain half is Corollary~\ref{cor:borel-chain} if $\wdt(P)<\infty$ and
Proposition~\ref{prop:infinite-width} otherwise. The antichain half is
Corollary~\ref{cor:borel-antichain}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-saturation} uses no definability of $G$ itself; all the
definability is concentrated in $\mathrm{(EC)}$, and is applied to the single
set $Y$. In particular Corollaries~\ref{cor:borel-chain}
and~\ref{cor:borel-antichain} hold for co-analytic partial orders as well,
granted $\mathrm{(EC)}$ for those, since a locked template and its active
blocks are finite objects. Taking $\mathcal A=\mathcal P(X)$ recovers the
Greene--Kleitman theorem for arbitrary orders of finite width, and its dual,
in the form of Aharoni and Korman~\cite{AharoniKorman1992}.
\end{remark}

\section{The prescription problem}
\label{sec:prescription}

Exact colouring in Theorem~\ref{thm:main-saturation} gives the optimal
norms without fixing the labels of the active blocks. We now ask when
prescribed labels can also be retained.

\subsection{Prescriptions and the finite obstruction}
\label{subsec:prescription}

\begin{definition}
\label{def:prescription}
Let $G$ be a graph on $X$ and $k\ge1$. A \emph{prescription} is a family
$E_0,\dots,E_{k-1}$ of pairwise disjoint independent subsets of $X$. It
\emph{extends} to a partition $X=A_0\sqcup\dots\sqcup A_{k-1}$ into
independent sets if $E_i\subseteq A_i$ for all $i<k$. We say the prescription
has the \emph{finite extension property} $\mathrm{(FE)}_k$ if for every finite
$F\subseteq X$ there is a partition $F=D_0\sqcup\dots\sqcup D_{k-1}$ into
$G\restr F$-independent sets with $F\cap E_i\subseteq D_i$ for all $i<k$.
\end{definition}

$\mathrm{(FE)}_k$ is plainly necessary for an extension to exist. It is also
sufficient, if no definability is demanded.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:compactness}
A prescription $E_0,\dots,E_{k-1}$ extends if and only if it satisfies
$\mathrm{(FE)}_k$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Necessity is immediate, on taking $D_i=F\cap A_i$. For sufficiency give
$k^X=\{0,\dots,k-1\}^X$ the product topology, so that it is compact, and
consider the closed sets $O_{x,y}=\{f:f(x)\neq f(y)\}$ for $x\,G\,y$ and
$O_{e,i}=\{f:f(e)=i\}$ for $i<k$ and $e\in E_i$. Given finitely many of them,
let $F$ be the finite set of points they mention, take
$F=D_0\sqcup\dots\sqcup D_{k-1}$ as in $\mathrm{(FE)}_k$ and let $f$ be any
extension of the map sending $x\in D_i$ to $i$. If $x\,G\,y$ with $x,y\in F$
then $x,y$ lie in different $D_i$, these being independent in $G\restr F$, so
$f(x)\neq f(y)$; and $f(e)=i$ for $e\in E_i\cap F$. Thus the family has the
finite intersection property, and any $f$ in the total intersection yields
$A_i=f^{-1}(\{i\})$.
\end{proof}

The remaining issue is Borel definability. For $G^{\perp}$ the prescribed
sets are chains, and Theorem~\ref{list-thm:main} below shows that
$\mathrm{(FE)}_k$ suffices for every finite $k$. For $G^{<}$ they are
antichains, and Theorem~\ref{thm:D} gives a negative answer at $k=3$.
We first give the two-colour argument, which applies to arbitrary Borel
$2$-colourable graphs and treats both kinds of prescription at once.

\subsection{Two blocks}
\label{subsec:two}

For $k=2$ the answer is affirmative, and the argument is a single cocycle
computation which sees neither chains nor antichains. Moreover only the weakest
form of $\mathrm{(FE)}_2$ is needed, namely its restriction to paths, which is
equivalent to a bare parity condition. Recall that a \emph{$G$-path of length
$n$} from $x$ to $y$ is a tuple $(x_0,\dots,x_n)$ with $x_0=x$, $x_n=y$ and
$x_m\,G\,x_{m+1}$ for $m<n$, and that $E_G$, the relation of being joined by a
$G$-path, is the connectedness relation of $G$, an analytic equivalence relation
when $G$ is Borel.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:two}
Let $G$ be a Borel graph on a standard Borel space $X$ admitting a Borel
proper $2$-colouring, and let $E_0,E_1\subseteq X$ be disjoint Borel sets. The
following are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $E_0,E_1$ extends to a partition of $X$ into two Borel
independent sets.
\item[(ii)] $E_0,E_1$ is a prescription satisfying $\mathrm{(FE)}_2$.
\item[(iii)] For every finite $F\subseteq X$ which is the vertex set of a
$G$-path there is a partition $F=D_0\sqcup D_1$ into
$G\restr F$-independent sets with $F\cap E_i\subseteq D_i$.
\item[(iv)] For all $i,j<2$, all $e\in E_i$ and $f\in E_j$ and every
$G$-path from $e$ to $f$, the length $n$ of the path satisfies
$n\equiv i+j\pmod 2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i)$\Rightarrow$(ii)$\Rightarrow$(iii) are restrictions, the first by
Proposition~\ref{prop:compactness}.

(iii)$\Rightarrow$(iv). Let $(x_0,\dots,x_n)$ be a path from $e\in E_i$ to
$f\in E_j$, let $F$ be its vertex set, and let $D_0,D_1$ be as in (iii); write
$d(x)=m$ for $x\in D_m$. Then $d$ takes two values and $d(x_m)\neq d(x_{m+1})$,
so $d(x_{m+1})=d(x_m)\oplus1$ and $j=d(f)=d(e)\oplus n=i\oplus n$, that is
$n\equiv i+j\pmod2$.

(iv)$\Rightarrow$(i). Note first that (iv) with $n=1$ says that $E_0$ and
$E_1$ are independent, and with $n=0$ that they are disjoint, so no further
hypothesis is needed. Fix a Borel proper $2$-colouring $c:X\to\{0,1\}$ of $G$;
it bears no relation to $E_0,E_1$, and we correct it by one global flip on each
connected component. Put
\[
\varepsilon(e)=c(e)\oplus i
\qquad (i<2,\ e\in E_i),
\]
a Borel map on $E=E_0\cup E_1$, well defined as $E_0\cap E_1=\varnothing$. We
claim that $\varepsilon$ is $E_G$-invariant. Let $e\in E_i$ and $f\in E_j$ be
joined by a path of length $n$. A proper $2$-colouring flips along every edge,
so $c(f)=c(e)\oplus n$, while $n=i\oplus j$ by (iv); hence
\[
\varepsilon(f)=c(f)\oplus j=c(e)\oplus n\oplus j
=c(e)\oplus i\oplus j\oplus j=\varepsilon(e).
\]
For $b<2$ set
\[
S_b=\{x\in X:\exists e\in E\ (x\,E_G\,e\ \wedge\ \varepsilon(e)=b)\}.
\]
Each $S_b$ is the projection of an analytic subset of $X^2$, hence analytic,
and both are $E_G$-invariant. If $x\in S_0\cap S_1$, witnessed by $e$ and $f$,
then $e\,E_G\,f$ while $\varepsilon(e)\neq\varepsilon(f)$, contrary to the
claim; so $S_0\cap S_1=\varnothing$. By the separation theorem for invariant
analytic sets~\cite{Burgess1979} (see also~\cite[Theorem~5.2.2]{Gao2009})
there is an $E_G$-invariant Borel $H$ with
$S_1\subseteq H\subseteq X\setminus S_0$.

Put $d=c\oplus\mathbf1_H$. If $x\,G\,y$ then $x\,E_G\,y$, so
$\mathbf1_H(x)=\mathbf1_H(y)$ while $c(x)\neq c(y)$, whence $d(x)\neq d(y)$ and
$A_m=d^{-1}(\{m\})$ are independent. If $e\in E_i$ then
$e\in S_{\varepsilon(e)}$ because $e\,E_G\,e$, so
$\mathbf1_H(e)=\varepsilon(e)$ by the choice of $H$ and
$d(e)=c(e)\oplus\varepsilon(e)=i$. Thus $E_i\subseteq A_i$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:two-orders}
Let $P$ be a Borel partial order on a standard Borel space $X$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(a)}] If $\wdt(P)\le2$, then every prescription by two Borel
chains satisfying $\mathrm{(FE)}_2$ extends to a Borel partition of $X$
into two chains.
\item[\textup{(b)}] If $\hgt(P)\le2$, then every prescription by two Borel
antichains satisfying $\mathrm{(FE)}_2$ extends to a Borel partition of
$X$ into two antichains.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
In case (a) apply Theorem~\ref{thm:two} to $G^{\perp}$, whose Borel proper
$2$-colourings are the Borel partitions of $X$ into two chains; one exists by
the Borel Dilworth theorem. In case (b) apply it to $G^{<}$, using the Borel
Mirsky theorem. Alternatively, in case (b) the colouring is elementary: the
analytic sets $\{x:\exists y\ x<_Py\}$ and $\{x:\exists y\ y<_Px\}$ are
disjoint because $\hgt(P)\le2$, so Luzin separation~\cite[\S14]{Kechris1995}
yields a Borel $B$ containing the first and disjoint from the second, and
$\mathbf1_{X\setminus B}$ is a Borel proper $2$-colouring of $G^{<}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:rotation}
The hypothesis that $G$ be Borel $2$-colourable cannot be dropped from
Theorem~\ref{thm:two}: with $E_0=E_1=\varnothing$ conditions (ii)--(iv) are
vacuous, and (i) is the hypothesis itself. And a $2$-colourable Borel graph
need not be Borel $2$-colourable, by Lemma~\ref{lem:rotation} below. In both
parts of Corollary~\ref{cor:two-orders} the hypothesis is supplied for free, so
the rotation graph is neither the comparability graph of a Borel order of
height two nor the incomparability graph of one of width two.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:two-only}
Two features of the number two are used, and both fail for more colours. A
proper $2$-colouring of a path is determined by its value at one endpoint,
which is what makes $\varepsilon$ well defined; and the ambiguity of the base
colouring on a connected component is a single flip, which is what makes the
correction an invariant Borel set. For $k\ge3$ these features are not available. The list-refinement
argument of \S\ref{sec:list-extension} uses the order structure instead
and preserves all prescribed colours simultaneously.
\end{remark}

\section{An anchored decomposition criterion}
\label{subsec:many}

We first give a sufficient condition for prescriptions in general Borel
graphs. The argument removes one independent set at a time while
preserving the remaining anchors.
Throughout this section $G$ is a Borel graph on $X$, $k\ge1$, and
$E_0,\dots,E_{k-1}$ are pairwise disjoint nonempty finite independent sets
with $\cl(X)\le k$. For $I\subseteq k$ put $E_I=\bigcup_{i\in I}E_i$.
Write $I_t=\{t,\dots,k-1\}$, so $|I_t|=k-t$ and $I_k=\varnothing$.

\begin{definition}
\label{def:AS-AD}
The prescription satisfies
\begin{itemize}
\item[$\mathrm{(AS)}$] \emph{(anchor saturation)} if for every $j<k$ and
every $y\in E_j$ there are $x_i\in E_i$ $(i<k)$ with $x_j=y$ such that
$\{x_i:i<k\}$ is a clique;
\item[$\mathrm{(AD)}$] \emph{(anchored decomposition)} if for every $t<k$
and every finite $Z$ with
\[
E_{I_t}\subseteq Z\subseteq X\setminus E_{k\setminus I_t}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\cl(Z)\le k-t,
\]
there is a partition $Z=\bigsqcup_{i\in I_t}P_i$ into independent sets
with $E_i\subseteq P_i$ for all $i\in I_t$.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

Taking $t=0$ in $\mathrm{(AD)}$, and observing that $\cl(Z)\le\cl(X)\le k$
always, we see that $\mathrm{(AD)}$ implies $\mathrm{(FE)}_k$. It is a
strengthening of $\mathrm{(FE)}_k$ to the tail index sets, and the counterexample of Theorem D shows that the converse can fail.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:AS-consequences}
Assume $\mathrm{(AS)}$. Then, for every nonempty $I\subseteq k$, every
$j\in I$ and every $y\in E_j$, there is a clique $A\subseteq E_I$ with
$y\in A$, $|A|=|I|$ and $|A\cap E_i|=1$ for $i\in I$. Consequently every
independent set containing some $E_i$ is disjoint from $E_j$ for all $j\neq i$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\{x_i:i<k\}$ be as in $\mathrm{(AS)}$; the $x_i$ are pairwise distinct,
lying in pairwise disjoint sets, so $A=\{x_i:i\in I\}$ is a clique of size
$|I|$ inside $E_I$ containing $y=x_j$ and meeting each $E_i$ $(i\in I)$ once.
For the second assertion, let $C$ be independent with $E_i\subseteq C$ and
suppose $y\in C\cap E_j$ with $j\neq i$. Applying the above with $I=\{i,j\}$
gives $x\in E_i\subseteq C$ adjacent to $y\in C$, contradicting independence.
\end{proof}

We isolate the definability input. A Borel independent $B\subseteq W$ is
\emph{finitely $m$-coherent in $W$} if every finite $H\subseteq W$ admits a
partition into at most $m$ independent sets one of which contains $H\cap B$.

\begin{itemize}
\item[$\mathrm{(PU)}$] \emph{Puncturing.} For every Borel $W\subseteq X$ with
$\cl(W)=m\ge1$ and every Borel independent $B\subseteq W$ which is finitely
$m$-coherent in $W$, there is a Borel independent $C$ with
$B\subseteq C\subseteq W$ meeting every clique of size $m$ contained in $W$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:PU-status}
For $\mathcal A=\mathcal P(X)$ the principle is a theorem: coherence is
condition $\mathrm{(FE)}_m$ for the prescription
$(B,\varnothing,\dots,\varnothing)$ in $W$, so
Proposition~\ref{prop:compactness} gives $W=A_0\sqcup\dots\sqcup A_{m-1}$ with
$B\subseteq A_0$, and a clique of size $m$ meets each $A_i$ at most once,
hence exactly once; take $C=A_0$. For incomparability graphs of finite-width Borel orders,
Theorem~\ref{list-thm:main} proves this assertion with $C$ Borel: use
the singleton list $\{0\}$ on $B$ and the full palette elsewhere,
then take the colour-$0$ class. For a general Borel graph, however,
$\mathrm{(PU)}$ remains an explicit hypothesis of the next theorem.
\end{remark}

\begin{example}
\label{ex:coherence-needed}
The coherence hypothesis in $\mathrm{(PU)}$ cannot be omitted, already on four
points. Let $G$ be the path $a-c-b-d$, so $\cl=2$, and let $B=\{a,d\}$; then
$B$ is independent, indeed maximal independent, yet $B$ misses the clique
$\{c,b\}$, so no independent $C\supseteq B$ meets every clique of size two.
Accordingly $B$ is not finitely $2$-coherent: the unique partition of the
vertex set into two independent sets is $\{a,b\}\sqcup\{c,d\}$, which separates
$a$ from $d$. In the language of orders, this is the four-element order given
by $a<b$, $a<d$, $c<d$, of width two, in which the only chain through $a$ and
$d$ is $\{a,d\}$ and it misses the antichain $\{b,c\}$.
\end{example}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:many}
Assume $\mathrm{(PU)}$, and let $E_0,\dots,E_{k-1}$ be a prescription by
finite sets satisfying $\mathrm{(AS)}$ and $\mathrm{(AD)}$, with
$\cl(X)\le k$. Then there are Borel independent sets $C_0,\dots,C_{k-1}$ with
\[
X=C_0\sqcup\dots\sqcup C_{k-1}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
E_i\subseteq C_i\quad(i<k).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $E=E_{k}$, set $X_0=X$ and, once $C_0,\dots,C_{t-1}$ have been chosen,
$X_t=X\setminus\bigcup_{s<t}C_s$. We maintain, for $t\le k$,
\begin{equation*}
X_t\ \text{is Borel},
\qquad
X_t\cap E=E_{I_t},
\qquad
\cl(X_t)=k-t.
\tag{$\ast_t$}
\label{eq:invariants}
\end{equation*}
At $t=0$ the first two are trivial, and $\cl(X)=k$ follows from $\cl(X)\le k$
together with Lemma~\ref{lem:AS-consequences} applied to $I=k$, which
produces a clique of size $k$; here we use that the $E_i$ are nonempty.

Assume \eqref{eq:invariants} at some $t<k$ and put $m=k-t\ge1$. Then
$E_t\subseteq X_t$, and $E_t$ is a finite, hence Borel, independent set.

\emph{Coherence.} Let $H\subseteq X_t$ be finite and put $Z=E_{I_t}\cup H$, a
finite set with $E_{I_t}\subseteq Z\subseteq X_t$. The $E_i$ being pairwise
disjoint, \eqref{eq:invariants} gives
\[
Z\cap E_{k\setminus I_t}\subseteq(X_t\cap E)\cap E_{k\setminus I_t}
=E_{I_t}\cap E_{k\setminus I_t}=\varnothing,
\]
so $Z\subseteq X\setminus E_{k\setminus I_t}$; and
$\cl(Z)\le\cl(X_t)=m=|I_t|$. Hypothesis $\mathrm{(AD)}$ therefore yields
$Z=\bigsqcup_{i\in I_t}P_i$ into independent sets with $E_i\subseteq P_i$, and
the sets $P_i\cap H$ $(i\in I_t)$ partition $H$ into at most $m$ independent
sets, the one indexed by $t$ containing $H\cap E_t$. Thus $E_t$ is finitely
$m$-coherent in $X_t$.

By $\mathrm{(PU)}$ there is a Borel independent $C_t$ with
$E_t\subseteq C_t\subseteq X_t$ meeting every clique of size $m$ contained in
$X_t$. Put $X_{t+1}=X_t\setminus C_t$, a Borel set.

\emph{The remaining blocks survive.} $C_t$ is independent and contains $E_t$,
so $C_t\cap E_j=\varnothing$ for $j\neq t$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:AS-consequences}; hence
\[
X_{t+1}\cap E=(X_t\cap E)\setminus C_t=E_{I_t}\setminus C_t=E_{I_{t+1}},
\]
the second invariant at $t+1$, and in particular
$E_{I_{t+1}}\subseteq X_{t+1}$.

\emph{The clique number drops.} A clique $K\subseteq X_{t+1}$ with $|K|\ge m$
would contain one of size exactly $m$, which lies in $X_t$ and therefore meets
$C_t$, contradicting $K\subseteq X_t\setminus C_t$; so $\cl(X_{t+1})\le m-1$.
If $m\ge2$, then $|I_{t+1}|=m-1\ge1$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:AS-consequences}
applied to $I_{t+1}$ produces a clique of size $m-1$ inside
$E_{I_{t+1}}\subseteq X_{t+1}$, so $\cl(X_{t+1})=m-1$. If $m=1$ then
$\cl(X_{t+1})=0$, and since singletons are cliques this means
$X_{t+1}=\varnothing$; in either case \eqref{eq:invariants} holds at $t+1$.

At $t=k$ we get $\cl(X_k)=0$, hence $X_k=\varnothing$, so the $C_t$ cover $X$;
they are pairwise disjoint because $C_t\subseteq X_t$ misses $C_s$ for $s<t$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:AD-content}
For $k\le2$ hypothesis $\mathrm{(AD)}$ is automatic given $\mathrm{(FE)}_k$:
the only tail index sets are $I_0=k$, for which $\mathrm{(AD)}$ is
$\mathrm{(FE)}_k$, and $I_{k-1}=\{k-1\}$, for which the requirement
$\cl(Z)\le1$ says that $Z$ is independent and the one-block partition serves.
Thus $\mathrm{(AD)}$ has additional content only when $k\ge3$.
\end{remark}

\section{Prescribed Borel chains}
\label{sec:list-extension}

\subsection{Lists and the auxiliary inputs}

Fix $n\ge1$. A list assignment is a Borel map
$L:X\to\mathcal P(n)$.
For a graph $G$ on $X$, a proper $L$-colouring is a map $c:X\to n$ with
$c(x)\in L(x)$ and $c(x)\ne c(y)$ whenever $xGy$. Call $L$ \emph{feasible}
if every finite induced graph admits a proper colouring from its lists.
By compactness of $n^X$, this is equivalent to the existence of an
abstract proper $L$-colouring. Indeed, all vertex-list and edge constraints
are closed finite-coordinate conditions in this product.

\begin{theorem}[Borel list extension]\label{list-thm:main}
Let $P=(X,\le_P)$ be a Borel partial order and $L:X\to\mathcal P(n)$ a
Borel list assignment. If $L$ is feasible for the incomparability graph of
$P$, then it has a Borel proper colouring.
\end{theorem}

An antichain of size $n+1$ would be a finite obstruction, so feasibility
implies $\wdt(P)\le n$.

\begin{corollary}[Prescribed Borel chains]\label{list-cor:prescription}
Let $E_0,\ldots,E_{n-1}$ be pairwise disjoint Borel subsets of a Borel partial
order. Suppose every finite $F\subseteq X$ has a partition into chains
$F=D_0\sqcup\cdots\sqcup D_{n-1}$ with $F\cap E_i\subseteq D_i$. Then
there are Borel chains $C_i\supseteq E_i$ with
$X=C_0\sqcup\cdots\sqcup C_{n-1}$. Conversely, such a partition plainly
implies the finite condition.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{list-thm:main} with $L(x)=\{i\}$ for $x\in E_i$, and
$L(x)=n$ otherwise. The finite condition ensures that each $E_i$
is a chain, and the resulting partition proves Theorem C.
\end{proof}

We use two results of Harrington, Marker, and Shelah
\cite{HarringtonMarkerShelah1988}. A thin Borel partial order is covered
by countably many Borel chains \cite[Theorem~5.1]{HarringtonMarkerShelah1988}.
Taking successive differences gives a Borel map $g:X\to\omega$ whose
fibres are chains. There are also a countable ordinal $\alpha$ and a
Borel injection $f:X\to2^\alpha$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{list-eq:hms}
x\le_P y\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f(x)\le_{\lex}f(y).
\end{equation}
Indeed, the construction of the order-preserving map in
\cite[Theorem~3.1 and its proof]{HarringtonMarkerShelah1988} separates
points not equivalent under the order; it is injective for a partial
order. Feasibility gives $\wdt(P)\le n$, so both results apply.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Compact-section projection and countable sections.}
A Borel subset of a product with a compact metrizable second factor,
all of whose sections are closed, has Borel projection. We use the
compact case of the Arsenin--Kunugui theorem
\cite[Theorem~35.46]{Kechris1995}; its projection formulation is also stated
explicitly in \cite[pp.~191, 193]{HolickyZeleny2000}. We also use the Lusin--Novikov theorem
\cite[Theorem~18.10]{Kechris1995}: a Borel relation with countable sections is
a countable union of graphs of partial Borel functions, and its projection
is Borel. At the end it supplies an enumeration of the fibres of a
countable-to-one Borel map.

\subsection{Separation and compact auxiliary data}

\begin{lemma}\label{list-lem:finite-cover}
If $V_0,\ldots,V_{k-1}$ are coanalytic subsets of a standard Borel space $T$
and $\bigcup_{i<k}V_i=T$, there is a Borel $h:T\to k$ with $t\in V_{h(t)}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $A_i=T\setminus V_i$. We prove by induction that finitely many analytic
sets with empty intersection have Borel supersets with empty intersection.
The one-set case is immediate. For the induction step, separate
$\bigcap_{i<k-1}A_i$ from $A_{k-1}$ by a Borel set $D$ containing the former
and avoiding the latter. On $T\setminus D$, apply induction to the first
$k-1$ sets, obtaining Borel hulls $B'_i$ with empty intersection. Then
$B_i=D\cup B'_i$ for $i<k-1$, together with $B_{k-1}=T\setminus D$, are the
required hulls. Now let $h(t)$ be the least $i$ with $t\notin B_i$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{list-lem:hull}
Let $Z$ be standard Borel, let $R_m\subseteq Z^m$ be analytic for every
$m\ge1$, and let $A\subseteq Z$ be analytic. If $R_m\cap A^m=\varnothing$
for all $m$, there is a Borel $B\supseteq A$ with
$R_m\cap B^m=\varnothing$ for all $m$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $m$. Enlarge the $m$ coordinate copies of $A$ one at a time. Having
chosen Borel $B_1,\ldots,B_{j-1}$ containing $A$, with $R_m$ disjoint from
$B_1\times\cdots\times B_{j-1}\times A^{m-j+1}$, project
\[
R_m\cap(B_1\times\cdots\times B_{j-1}\times Z\times A^{m-j})
\]
onto coordinate $j$. This projection is analytic and disjoint from $A$.
Analytic separation gives a Borel $B_j\supseteq A$ avoiding it. At the end,
$R_m$ avoids $(B^{(m)})^m$ for $B^{(m)}=\bigcap_{j=1}^m B_j$.
Take $B=\bigcap_{m\ge1}B^{(m)}$.
\end{proof}

We shall use this lemma for \emph{forbidden} colours rather than for
positive assignments. This distinction allows whole colour lists to be
refined without selecting one colour at a time.

\begin{corollary}[Borel hull for feasible bans]\label{list-cor:bans}
Let $G$ be Borel and $L$ a feasible Borel assignment from $n$. Let
$A\subseteq X\times n$ be analytic, and suppose there is an abstract
proper $L$-colouring avoiding all bans $(x,i)\in A$. Then there is a Borel
$B\supseteq A$ such that
\[
L'(x)=L(x)\setminus\{i:(x,i)\in B\}
\]
is feasible.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $R_m$ consist of $m$-tuples of bans which, when added to the original
$G,L$ constraints, admit no full colouring. By compactness failure has a
finite vertex witness. For fixed finitely many vertices and bans,
nonexistence of a colouring is a finite Boolean combination of Borel
conditions, because the palette is fixed and finite. Existential
quantification over the finite witness makes $R_m$ analytic. The assumed
colouring makes $R_m\cap A^m$ empty. Apply Lemma~\ref{list-lem:hull}.
Every finite collection of bans in the resulting $B$ is feasible together
with all original constraints. Compactness in $n^X$ then gives a full
colouring avoiding $B$. In particular, no resulting vertex list is empty.
\end{proof}

We next isolate the uniform compactness argument. In its statement, a
\emph{Borel family of closed finite-coordinate constraints} means the
following. There are countably many constraint types. For a type of arity
$r$, the indices form a Borel subset of
\[
\{(t,x_1,\ldots,x_r):x_j\in X_t\},
\]
and satisfaction is a Borel predicate in the index, an auxiliary parameter
$k\in H$, and the $r$ colours. For each fixed index and fixed colour word,
the satisfying set of $k$ is closed in $H$. Equality of colours at repeated
vertices is understood. Several types can have the same arity.

\begin{lemma}[Selection of compact auxiliary data]\label{list-lem:compact-choice}
Let $T,Z$ be standard Borel, $W\subseteq T\times Z$ Borel, and
$X_t=\{x:(t,x)\in W\}$. Let $H$ be compact metrizable. Suppose a Borel
family of closed finite-coordinate constraints has a solution
\[
(k,d)\in H\times n^{X_t}
\]
for every $t$. There is a Borel $k:T\to H$ such that for every $t$ some
full assignment $d$ satisfies all constraints with auxiliary parameter
$k(t)$. No Borel choice of that full assignment is asserted.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a compact $C\subseteq H$, let $V_C$ be the parameters for which a full
solution exists with $k\in C$. We claim that $V_C$ is coanalytic. The
solution set is closed in the compact product $H\times n^{X_t}$.
Thus failure with $k\in C$ is witnessed by finitely many constraint
instances. For a fixed tuple of instances only finitely many vertex
colourings need be considered. For each such colouring, the possible
$k\in C$ form a compact section of a Borel relation. The compact-section
projection theorem makes its nonemptiness a Borel condition. Finite
unions over colourings therefore make the finite satisfiability test
Borel. Quantifying existentially over the finite tuple of constraint
indices makes $T\setminus V_C$ analytic, as claimed.

Choose a finitely branching tree of nonempty compact subsets $C_s$ of
$H$, with root $H$, each node covered by its children, children contained
in their parent, and diameters tending to zero with the level. Such a
tree is obtained by finite closed-ball covers of compact sets. For each
$t$ choose a viable child at every step. This can be done Borelly using
Lemma~\ref{list-lem:finite-cover}: on the Borel set of parameters which have
arrived at a given node, the coanalytic viability sets of its finitely
many children cover that set. There are only finitely many nodes at each
level. The selected nested compact sets have one-point intersection
$\{k(t)\}$, and the limit map $t\mapsto k(t)$ is Borel.

For fixed $t$, the full solution sets with the selected successive compact
restrictions are nonempty nested closed subsets of
$H\times n^{X_t}$. Their intersection is nonempty by compactness, and its
auxiliary coordinate is $k(t)$. This verifies all constraints, including
those involving unmarked vertices. Empty $X_t$ present no difficulty.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A countable-range refinement lemma}

Fix $P$, write $G$ for its incomparability graph, and take a Borel map
$g:X\to\omega$ whose fibres are chains. The lists need not respect $g$.

For a Borel map $q:X\to T$ and lists $L$, the invariant to be preserved is
\begin{equation*}\label{list-eq:sep}
xGy\ \text{ and }\ q(x)\ne q(y)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad L(x)\cap L(y)=\varnothing.
\tag{S}
\end{equation*}
Thus any remaining potential same-colour edge is confined to a single
$q$-fibre. A Borel map need not be onto its standard Borel codomain.

\begin{lemma}[Countable-range refinement]\label{list-lem:refine}
Suppose $q:X\to T$ and $\rho:X\to[0,1]$ are Borel, $L$ is Borel and
feasible, and \eqref{list-eq:sep} holds. Assume
\begin{equation*}
q(x)=q(y),\ x\le_P y\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\rho(x)\le\rho(y).
\end{equation*}
There are a Borel refinement $q':X\to T'$, feasible Borel sublists
$L'\subseteq L$, and Borel functions $a_m:T'\to[0,1]$, $m\in\N$, such that
\begin{align}
xGy,\ q'(x)\ne q'(y)&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad L'(x)\cap L'(y)=\varnothing,
\label{list-eq:new-sep}\\
\rho\bigl((q')^{-1}(\{t'\})\bigr)&\subseteq\{a_m(t'):m\in\N\}.
\label{list-eq:enum}
\end{align}
Refinement means $q'(x)=q'(y)\Rightarrow q(x)=q(y)$. The sequence in
\eqref{list-eq:enum} may contain repetitions and values not attained in the
fibre.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\noindent\emph{Compact profiles.}
Let $\Kz$ be the hyperspace of nonempty compact subsets of $[0,1]$, with
its usual Hausdorff metric topology, together with an isolated point
representing the empty set. The following countable product is compact
metrizable:
\[
H=(\Kz)^{n\times\omega}.
\]
A coordinate $K_{ij}$ will record a closed profile for target colour $i$
on background chain $g^{-1}(\{j\})$.
For real numbers $u,v$ write
$I(u,v)=(\min\{u,v\},\max\{u,v\})$; it is empty if $u=v$.

For $X_t=q^{-1}(\{t\})$, consider pairs $(K,d)\in H\times n^{X_t}$
satisfying the proper $L$-colouring constraints and
\begin{align}
\rho(x)&\in K_{d(x),g(x)} &&(x\in X_t),\notag\\
K_{d(x),g(y)}\cap I(\rho(x),\rho(y))&=\varnothing
&&(x,y\in X_t,\ xGy).\label{list-eq:robust}
\end{align}
The second condition is imposed on \emph{ordered} incomparable pairs,
so both directions are included. These are Borel finite-coordinate
constraints, closed in $K$ for fixed vertices and colours: hyperspace
membership is closed, and avoiding a fixed open interval is closed.
Joint dependence on the interval endpoints is Borel (in fact the
corresponding hit relation is open).

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Existence of a full joint solution.}
Fix any abstract proper $L$-colouring $d$ of $X_t$, and set
\begin{equation*}
K_{ij}=\operatorname{cl}\{\rho(z):z\in X_t,\ d(z)=i,\ g(z)=j\}.
\end{equation*}
We verify \eqref{list-eq:robust}. Suppose $xGy$, $\rho(x)<\rho(y)$, and the
indicated profile meets the open interval. By the definition of closure,
there is an actual $z\in X_t$ with
\[
d(z)=d(x),\qquad g(z)=g(y),\qquad
\rho(x)<\rho(z)<\rho(y).
\]
The first equality makes $x,z$ comparable, and the second makes $z,y$
comparable. Local monotonicity forces $x<_P z<_P y$, contradicting
$xGy$. If $\rho(y)<\rho(x)$, the same reasoning yields
$y<_P z<_P x$, also a contradiction. This verifies the constraints.
Consequently Lemma~\ref{list-lem:compact-choice} gives Borelly chosen profiles
$K_{ij}(t)$ admitting a full joint solution in every fibre.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Borel sublists which respect the profiles.}
Ban a target colour $i$ at $x$ if either
\[
\rho(x)\notin K_{i,g(x)}(q(x)),
\]
or there exists $y$ with $q(y)=q(x)$, $xGy$, and
\[
K_{i,g(y)}(q(x))\cap I(\rho(x),\rho(y))\ne\varnothing.
\]
The set $A\subseteq X\times n$ of these bans is analytic: the first
condition is Borel and the second is the projection of a Borel relation.
Every $q$-fibre admits an original listed colouring avoiding all these
bans, by the selected joint solution. For finitely many vertices, choose
such a colouring separately in each of the finitely many fibres involved.
Across different fibres \eqref{list-eq:sep} prevents a monochromatic edge.
Thus the global list constraints together with all bans are finitely
satisfiable, and compactness gives a full solution. Apply
Corollary~\ref{list-cor:bans} to obtain feasible Borel sublists $L'\subseteq L$
which avoid all bans.

It follows that every allowed pair $(x,i)$ satisfies both profile
conditions, regardless of whether $i$ is eventually chosen at $x$.
This universal property of an allowed colour is what will prove
\eqref{list-eq:new-sep}.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Uniformly countable exceptional values.}
For a compact $K\subseteq[0,1]$ and $r\in\Q\cap[0,1]$, consider
\[
d_r^-(K)=\max(K\cap[0,r]),\qquad
d_r^+(K)=\min(K\cap[r,1]),
\]
using $0$ when the relevant intersection is empty. These are Borel
functions of $K$. For example, nonemptiness of an intersection with a
fixed closed interval is Borel, and the threshold tests for its minimum
and maximum are hit tests for fixed half-open intervals. Such an interval
is a countable union of compact intervals, so these hit tests are Borel.
Let $D_t$ be the countable set of all these values, for every rational
$r$ and every profile $K_{ij}(t)$ with $i<n$, $j<\omega$. It has an
enumeration by
total Borel functions $d_m(t)$, and
$\{(t,u):u\in D_t\}=\bigcup_m\{(t,d_m(t))\}$ is Borel.

The set $D_t$ contains every point of any $K_{ij}(t)$ which is isolated
from one side in that compact set. For a right-isolated $u<1$, choose a
rational $r>u$ in the gap to obtain $u=d_r^-(K_{ij}(t))$; $u=1$ is handled
by $r=1$. Left isolation is dual, with $r=0$ handling $u=0$.
Extra values introduced by the default convention are harmless.

Define the new labels in the standard Borel space
$T'=T\times(\{\ast\}\sqcup[0,1])$ by
\begin{equation*}
q'(x)=
\begin{cases}
(q(x),\ast),&\rho(x)\in D_{q(x)},\\
(q(x),\rho(x)),&\rho(x)\notin D_{q(x)}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation*}
In an exceptional part the $\rho$-range is contained in $D_t$; in an
ordinary part it is a singleton. Thus use
$a_m(t,\ast)=d_m(t)$ and $a_m(t,u)=u$ to obtain \eqref{list-eq:enum}.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Every possible same-colour edge stays in a new part.}
Suppose $xGy$ and $i\in L'(x)\cap L'(y)$. The old separation invariant
implies $q(x)=q(y)=t$. If $\rho(x)=\rho(y)$, their new labels agree.
Otherwise suppose $\rho(x)<\rho(y)$. Membership at $x$, together with
\eqref{list-eq:robust} for the allowed pair $(y,i)$, gives
\[
\rho(x)\in K_{i,g(x)}(t),\qquad
K_{i,g(x)}(t)\cap(\rho(x),\rho(y))=\varnothing.
\]
Thus $\rho(x)$ is right-isolated in this profile. Similarly,
$\rho(y)$ is left-isolated in $K_{i,g(y)}(t)$. Both belong to $D_t$, so
$q'(x)=q'(y)=(t,\ast)$. This proves \eqref{list-eq:new-sep} and the lemma.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Countable transfinite reduction to countable fibres}

We give the construction in full, including successor coding and the
preservation of feasibility at countable limit stages.

\begin{proposition}\label{list-prop:countable-parts}
Let $P$ admit a Borel map $g:X\to\omega$ with chain fibres and an injective
Borel map $f:X\to2^\alpha$ satisfying \eqref{list-eq:hms}, for countable
$\alpha$. For every feasible finite-palette Borel $L$, there are feasible
Borel sublists $L_\ast\subseteq L$ and a countable-to-one Borel map
$q_\ast:X\to T_\ast$ satisfying \eqref{list-eq:sep} for $L_\ast,q_\ast$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For every $\beta\le\alpha$ construct Borel maps
$q_\beta:X\to T_\beta$ and $u_\beta:X\to[0,1]$, Borel feasible lists
$L_\beta\subseteq L$, and total Borel functions
$a_m^\beta:T_\beta\to[0,1]$, so that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] the $q_\beta$ refine one another and the $L_\beta$ decrease;
\item[(b)] \eqref{list-eq:sep} holds for $q_\beta,L_\beta$;
\item[(c)] inside a $q_\beta$-fibre, $u_\beta$ exactly represents the
lexicographic order of $f\restr\beta$: for such $x,y$,
\begin{equation}\label{list-eq:prefix-order}
u_\beta(x)\le u_\beta(y)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
f(x)\restr\beta\le_{\lex}f(y)\restr\beta;
\end{equation}
\item[(d)]
$u_\beta(q_\beta^{-1}(\{t\}))\subseteq
\{a_m^\beta(t):m\in\N\}$.
\end{enumerate}
All target spaces are standard Borel. Enumerations may repeat and contain
unused values. In particular \eqref{list-eq:prefix-order} asserts equality of
real values exactly when the corresponding prefixes are equal in a fibre.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Initial stage.}
Take $q_0$ constant, $u_0=0$, $L_0=L$, and $a_m^0=0$. All invariants hold.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Successor stage.}
Keep $q_{\beta+1}=q_\beta$ and $L_{\beta+1}=L_\beta$.
For $t\in T_\beta$ and $r\in[0,1]$ put
\begin{align*}
A_t(r)&=\sum_{m\ge0}2^{-m-1}\ind_{\{a_m^\beta(t)<r\}},\\
M_t(r)&=\sum_{m\ge0}2^{-m-1}\ind_{\{a_m^\beta(t)=r\}},\\
F_t(r,b)&=A_t(r)+\frac{1+b}{3}M_t(r)\qquad(b\in\{0,1\}).
\end{align*}
These functions are Borel. Every actual $u_\beta$-value has strictly
positive mass $M_t(r)$. For actual values $r<s$,
\[
F_t(r,b)<A_t(r)+M_t(r)\le A_t(s)<F_t(s,b'),
\]
and $F_t(r,0)<F_t(r,1)$. Thus $F_t$ inserts two ordered values strictly
inside a positive interval assigned to each old value; it respects the
lexicographic order of the old value followed by one bit, even when the
countable old range is dense.
Define
\begin{equation*}
u_{\beta+1}(x)=F_{q_\beta(x)}\bigl(u_\beta(x),f(x)(\beta)\bigr).
\end{equation*}
This verifies (c). Enumerate
$F_t(a_m^\beta(t),b)$ for $(m,b)\in\N\times\{0,1\}$ to obtain (d).
Repetitions in the old enumeration do not affect positivity or the order
inequalities. The other invariants are unchanged.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Limit stage.}
Let $0<\lambda\le\alpha$ be a limit ordinal. First form
\begin{equation*}
q^-_\lambda(x)=(q_\beta(x))_{\beta<\lambda},\qquad
L^-_\lambda(x)=\bigcap_{\beta<\lambda}L_\beta(x).
\end{equation*}
The target of $q^-_\lambda$ is a countable product of standard Borel
spaces. Both objects are Borel.

We check feasibility explicitly. For a finite $F\subseteq X$, each ban
$(x,i)$ with $x\in F$ and $i\notin L^-_\lambda(x)$ occurs at some earlier
stage. There are only finitely many such pairs. Therefore one
$\beta_0<\lambda$ contains all these bans and
\[
L^-_\lambda(x)=L_{\beta_0}(x)\qquad(x\in F).
\]
A colouring witnessing feasibility at that stage colours $F$ from its
limit lists. Hence $L^-_\lambda$ is feasible. If an edge has a colour
allowed at both ends by $L^-_\lambda$, invariant (b) puts its endpoints
in the same $q_\beta$-fibre for every $\beta<\lambda$. Thus the separation
invariant also holds for $q^-_\lambda,L^-_\lambda$.

Choose an increasing cofinal sequence $(\beta_m)_{m\ge0}$ in $\lambda$ and
set
\begin{equation*}
v_\lambda(x)=\sum_{m\ge0}2^{-m-1}u_{\beta_m}(x).
\end{equation*}
Inside a fixed $q^-_\lambda$-fibre, this exactly represents the order of
$f\restr\lambda$. Indeed, equal prefixes give equal values in every
summand. For distinct prefixes let $\delta<\lambda$ be the first
coordinate of disagreement. All summand inequalities have the same
orientation, by (c), and every summand with $\beta_m>\delta$ is strict.
The positive weighted sum is therefore strictly ordered in the required
direction. In particular $v_\lambda$ is nondecreasing on $P$ within each
$q^-_\lambda$-fibre.

Apply Lemma~\ref{list-lem:refine} to $q^-_\lambda,L^-_\lambda,v_\lambda$.
Let $q_\lambda,L_\lambda$ be its outputs, put $u_\lambda=v_\lambda$,
and use its enumerating functions for $a_m^\lambda$. The refinement
preserves the exact prefix comparison, since it only splits fibres.
All four invariants follow.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Termination.}
There are only countably many stages, so the construction uses only
countably many Borel choices, products and intersections; each of the
constructed maps and lists is Borel. At stage $\alpha$, (c) and the
injectivity of $f$ make $u_\alpha$ injective within each $q_\alpha$-fibre.
By (d), that fibre is countable. Take
$q_\ast=q_\alpha$ and $L_\ast=L_\alpha$. The case $\alpha=0$ is included:
then injectivity forces $|X|\le1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{list-lem:finish}
Let $G$ be Borel, let $q:X\to T$ be Borel and countable-to-one, and let
$L$ be feasible Borel finite-palette lists satisfying \eqref{list-eq:sep}.
Then $G$ has a Borel proper $L$-colouring.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lusin--Novikov, $q(X)$ is Borel and there are total Borel maps
$b_m:q(X)\to X$ enumerating every fibre. To obtain total maps, first
choose the value of the least defined partial enumerator, and use it to
fill undefined entries in the other enumerators. Finite fibres are thus
padded with repetitions.

For $t\in q(X)$ the allowed sequences in $n^\N$ must assign equal colours
to repeated vertices, different colours to adjacent vertices, and a colour
from $L(b_m(t))$ at coordinate $m$. This is a nonempty compact set.
A finite word $s$ is extendable precisely when for every $k\ge |s|$ there
is a colouring of the first $k$ coordinates extending $s$ and meeting all
these finite constraints. This condition is Borel in $t$: for each $k$
there are finitely many possible words. Recursively select the least
extendable next digit. The resulting full sequence $c_t$ depends Borelly
on $t$. Colour $x$ by $c_{q(x)}(m)$ at the least $m$ with $b_m(q(x))=x$.
This least-index function is Borel, and repetition constraints ensure
consistency. The colouring is proper inside each fibre and respects $L$.
If $xGy$ lie in different fibres, their lists are disjoint by
\eqref{list-eq:sep}, so the same conclusion holds across fibres.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{list-thm:main}]
The empty space is immediate. Feasibility gives $\wdt(P)\le n$.
The Harrington--Marker--Shelah theorems give a Borel map to $\omega$
with chain fibres and an injective monotone lexicographic
representation. Apply Proposition~\ref{list-prop:countable-parts}
and then Lemma~\ref{list-lem:finish}. The resulting colouring
respects $L$.
\end{proof}

Taking $L(x)=n$ for every $x$ gives the finite Borel Dilworth
theorem as a special case of Theorem~\ref{list-thm:main}, since
finite Dilworth makes these lists feasible whenever
$\wdt(P)\le n$.

\subsection{Preserving the saturation template}
\label{sec:preserve-template}

We apply Theorem C to the active blocks of the finite template
from Section~\ref{sec:saturation}.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:preserve-locked}
Let $P$ be a Borel partial order of finite width, fix $r\ge1$, and let
$U,V,S,\mathcal E$ be as in \S\ref{sec:saturation}, applied to the
incomparability graph of $P$. In particular, $\mathcal E$ is a locked
chain template on the finite set $S$.
There is a finite Borel chain partition $\mathcal C$ of $X$ which extends
$\mathcal E$ and is simultaneously $r$- and $(r+1)$-saturated.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $E_0,\ldots,E_{\ell-1}$ be the active blocks of $\mathcal E$ and put
\[
Y=(X\setminus S)\cup\bigcup_{i<\ell}E_i.
\]
If $\ell=0$, Lemma~\ref{lem:active-decomposition} gives $X=S$, and the
claim follows from \eqref{eq:template-norms}.
Suppose $\ell>0$. For every finite $F\subseteq X\setminus S$,
Lemma~\ref{lem:active-decomposition} partitions $F$ into sets $F_i$ such
that each $E_i\cup F_i$ is a chain. Consequently the prescription
$E_0,\ldots,E_{\ell-1}$ on $Y$ satisfies $\mathrm{(FE)}_\ell$.
By Corollary~\ref{list-cor:prescription}, there are Borel chains
\[
Y=C_0\sqcup\cdots\sqcup C_{\ell-1},\qquad E_i\subseteq C_i.
\]
Since $Y\cap S=\bigcup_{i<\ell}E_i$ and the parts are disjoint,
$C_i\cap S=E_i$. Retain every inactive block of $\mathcal E$ as a separate
part. The resulting finite Borel chain partition $\mathcal C$ therefore
extends $\mathcal E$.
For $t\in\{r,r+1\}$, every active block has at least $t$ elements by
Lemma~\ref{lem:active-points}. Thus $|C_i|\wedge t=|E_i|\wedge t=t$,
and the inactive parts are unchanged. Equation~\eqref{eq:template-norms}
now gives
\[
\lVert\mathcal C\rVert_r=\alpha_r(X),\qquad
\lVert\mathcal C\rVert_{r+1}=\alpha_{r+1}(X).
\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\section{Three antichains: a Borel obstruction}
\label{subsec:counterexample}

We now prove Theorem D. The construction encodes an ergodic obstruction into a
height-three order; we begin by recalling the obstruction.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:rotation}
Let $\T=\R/\Z$, fix an irrational $\alpha$, let $T(x)=x+\alpha$ and let
$G_T=\{(x,y)\in\T^2:y=T^{\pm1}(x)\}$. Then $G_T$ has chromatic number $2$ and
Borel chromatic number $3$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The rotation acts freely, so every connected component of $G_T$ is a two-way
infinite path; hence $G_T$ is acyclic and $2$-colourable, by alternating along
each orbit. If $c$ were a Borel proper $2$-colouring and $A=c^{-1}(\{0\})$,
then $A+\alpha=\T\setminus A$, hence $A+2\alpha=A$; rotation by $2\alpha$ is
ergodic for Lebesgue measure $\lambda$, so $\lambda(A)\in\{0,1\}$, whereas
$\lambda(A)=\lambda(A+\alpha)$ and $\lambda(A)+\lambda(A+\alpha)=1$ force
$\lambda(A)=\tfrac12$. That the Borel chromatic number is exactly $3$ is
classical~\cite{KechrisSoleckiTodorcevic1999}; only the lower bound is used
below.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:D}
There are a Borel partial order $P=(X,\le_P)$ of height three on a standard
Borel space and a prescription $E_0,E_1,E_2$ by three-element $P$-antichains
such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(i)}] the prescription extends to a partition of $X$ into
three antichains, hence satisfies $\mathrm{(FE)}_3$;
\item[\textup{(ii)}] the prescription satisfies $\mathrm{(AS)}$;
\item[\textup{(iii)}] no \emph{Borel} partition of $X$ into three antichains
extends the prescription.
\end{enumerate}
Moreover $\mathrm{(AD)}$ fails for the prescription, on a seven-element set.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{The point set.} Let $\T$, $\alpha$ and $T$ be as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:rotation}. Put
\[
X=\{e_0,e_1,e_2\}\cup\{q_0,q_1,q_2\}
\cup\{t_j^{\,1},t_j^{\,2}:j<3\}
\cup\bigl(\T\times\{v,b,c,p,r,s\}\bigr),
\]
writing $v_x,b_x,c_x,p_x,r_x,s_x$ for the six points of the fibre over
$x\in\T$. We call $e_0,e_1,e_2$ the \emph{anchors}, $q_0,q_1,q_2$ the
\emph{references}, $t_j^{\,1},t_j^{\,2}$ the \emph{saturating points}, and the
fibre points the \emph{gadget points}. As a disjoint union of a finite set and
six copies of $\T$, $X$ is standard Borel.

\emph{The order.} Let $\le_P$ be the reflexive transitive closure of the
following strict relations, for $i,j<3$ with $i\neq j$ and all $x\in\T$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(I)] $e_j<q_i$;
\item[(II)] $v_x<q_2$, \quad $b_x<q_1$, \quad $c_x<q_0$;
\item[(III)] $e_1<p_x$, \quad $e_2<r_x$, \quad $e_0<s_x$;
\item[(IV)] $v_x<r_x$, \quad $v_{T(x)}<p_x$, \quad $v_{T(x)}<s_x$, \quad
$b_x<r_x$, \quad $b_x<s_x$, \quad $c_x<p_x$, \quad $c_x<r_x$;
\item[(V)] $e_j<t_j^{\,1}<t_j^{\,2}$.
\end{enumerate}
All the displayed relations are Borel, $T$ being a homeomorphism, so $\le_P$
is Borel. The relations (I)--(IV) go from the set
$\Lambda_0=\{e_0,e_1,e_2\}\cup\{v_x,b_x,c_x:x\in\T\}$ to the set
$\Lambda_2=\{q_0,q_1,q_2\}\cup\{p_x,r_x,s_x:x\in\T\}$, and no point of
$\Lambda_2$ lies below anything; the relations (V) attach, above each anchor,
a two-element chain comparable to nothing else. Hence the transitive closure
adds only the pairs $e_j<t_j^{\,2}$, every $\le_P$-chain is contained either
in $\Lambda_0\cup\Lambda_2$, where it has at most two elements, or in
$\{e_j,t_j^{\,1},t_j^{\,2}\}$, and
\[
\hgt(P)=3,
\]
the chain $e_0<t_0^{\,1}<t_0^{\,2}$ realising the height. Note also that the
anchors are pairwise incomparable and $\le_P$-minimal.

\emph{The prescription.} Let $\pi_j:\{1,2\}\to3\setminus\{j\}$ be the
increasing bijection, so
$\pi_0=(1,2)\mapsto(1,2)$, $\pi_1=(1,2)\mapsto(0,2)$ and
$\pi_2=(1,2)\mapsto(0,1)$, and put
\[
E_i=\{e_i\}\cup\{t_j^{\,n}:j<3,\ n\in\{1,2\},\ \pi_j(n)=i\},
\]
that is
\[
E_0=\{e_0,\,t_1^{\,1},\,t_2^{\,1}\},\qquad
E_1=\{e_1,\,t_0^{\,1},\,t_2^{\,2}\},\qquad
E_2=\{e_2,\,t_0^{\,2},\,t_1^{\,2}\}.
\]
These are pairwise disjoint, of size three, and are antichains: the anchors
are pairwise incomparable, a saturating point $t_j^{\,n}$ is comparable only
to $e_j$ and to the other saturating point over $j$, and $\pi_j(n)\neq j$
while $\pi_j(1)\neq\pi_j(2)$, so no two members of a single $E_i$ are
comparable. Figures~\ref{fig:gadget} and~\ref{fig:saturation} display,
respectively, the gadget over a single $x\in\T$ and the saturating chains.

\emph{Forcing.} Let $d:X\to3$ be any proper colouring of the comparability
graph of $P$ with $d(e_i)=i$ for $i<3$. By (I) the point $q_i$ lies above the
two anchors whose colours are the elements of $3\setminus\{i\}$, so
\[
d(q_i)=i\qquad(i<3).
\]
Combining this with (II) and (III) confines $d$ on the gadget points to the
domains
\begin{gather*}
d(v_x)\in\{0,1\},\qquad d(b_x)\in\{0,2\},\qquad d(c_x)\in\{1,2\},\\
d(p_x)\in\{0,2\},\qquad d(r_x)\in\{0,1\},\qquad d(s_x)\in\{1,2\},
\end{gather*}
for every $x\in\T$. We claim that these domains together with (IV) force
\begin{equation}
d(v_{T(x)})=1-d(v_x)\qquad(x\in\T).
\label{eq:forcing}
\end{equation}

Suppose $d(v_x)=0$. From $v_x<r_x$ and $d(r_x)\in\{0,1\}$ we get $d(r_x)=1$;
from $c_x<r_x$ and $d(c_x)\in\{1,2\}$, $d(c_x)=2$; from $c_x<p_x$ and
$d(p_x)\in\{0,2\}$, $d(p_x)=0$; and from $v_{T(x)}<p_x$ and
$d(v_{T(x)})\in\{0,1\}$, $d(v_{T(x)})=1$.

Suppose $d(v_x)=1$. From $v_x<r_x$, $d(r_x)=0$; from $b_x<r_x$ and
$d(b_x)\in\{0,2\}$, $d(b_x)=2$; from $b_x<s_x$ and $d(s_x)\in\{1,2\}$,
$d(s_x)=1$; and from $v_{T(x)}<s_x$, $d(v_{T(x)})=0$. This proves
\eqref{eq:forcing}.

\emph{(iii).} Suppose $d$ as above is Borel. Then $f:\T\to\{0,1\}$,
$f(x)=d(v_x)$, is Borel, and \eqref{eq:forcing} says that $f$ is a proper
$2$-colouring of $G_T$, contradicting Lemma~\ref{lem:rotation}. Since a Borel
partition $X=A_0\sqcup A_1\sqcup A_2$ into antichains with $E_i\subseteq A_i$
yields such a $d$, namely $d(x)=i$ for $x\in A_i$, no such partition exists.

\emph{(i).} By Lemma~\ref{lem:rotation} choose a proper $2$-colouring
$f:\T\to\{0,1\}$ of $G_T$, so $f(T(x))=1-f(x)$. Define $d$ on $X$ by
$d(e_i)=d(q_i)=i$, by $d(t_j^{\,n})=\pi_j(n)$, and on the fibre over $x$
according to the table
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
d(v_x)=f(x) & b_x & c_x & p_x & r_x & s_x\\\hline
0 & 0 & 2 & 0 & 1 & 2\\
1 & 2 & 1 & 2 & 0 & 1
\end{array}
\]
We check that $d$ is proper. The comparable pairs are those listed in
(I)--(V) together with $e_j<t_j^{\,2}$. For (I), $d(e_j)=j\neq i=d(q_i)$. For
(V) and $e_j<t_j^{\,2}$, the three points $e_j,t_j^{\,1},t_j^{\,2}$ receive the
three distinct values $j,\pi_j(1),\pi_j(2)$. The entries in the table
lie in the colour domains imposed by (II) and (III), and direct
inspection gives different colours at the endpoints of each relation
in (IV). For the two relations involving $v_{T(x)}$, use
$d(v_{T(x)})=f(T(x))=1-f(x)$; it differs from both $d(p_x)$ and
$d(s_x)$ in each row. Thus $d$ is proper. Since
$d(e_i)=i$ and $d(t_j^{\,n})=\pi_j(n)$, its colour classes extend
$E_0,E_1,E_2$. Restriction gives $\mathrm{(FE)}_3$.

\emph{(ii).} Fix $j<3$. The chain $e_j<t_j^{\,1}<t_j^{\,2}$ has three
elements and meets $E_j$ in $e_j$ and each $E_i$ with $i\neq j$ in the single
point $t_j^{\,\pi_j^{-1}(i)}$. This one chain witnesses $\mathrm{(AS)}$ for
$e_j$ and for both saturating points above $e_j$; as $j$ ranges over $3$ all
nine points of $E_0\cup E_1\cup E_2$ are covered.

\emph{Failure of $\mathrm{(AD)}$.} Take $t=1$, so $I_1=\{1,2\}$, and
\[
Z=E_1\cup E_2\cup\{q_0\}
=\{e_1,t_0^{\,1},t_2^{\,2},e_2,t_0^{\,2},t_1^{\,2},q_0\},
\]
a seven-element set. Then $E_{I_1}\subseteq Z$ and $Z\cap E_0=\varnothing$,
since $e_0,t_1^{\,1},t_2^{\,1}\notin Z$. Moreover $\hgt(P\restr Z)=2$: the
only points of $Z$ lying strictly above something in $Z$ are $t_0^{\,2}$,
$t_1^{\,2}$, $t_2^{\,2}$ and $q_0$, and nothing of $Z$ lies above any of
these, while $t_0^{\,1}$ has nothing of $Z$ below it, its only lower bound
being $e_0$. Thus the clique number of $G^{<}\restr Z$ equals $2=|I_1|$, so
$\mathrm{(AD)}$ would provide antichains $P_1\supseteq E_1$ and
$P_2\supseteq E_2$ partitioning $Z$. But $e_1<q_0$ and $e_2<q_0$ by (I), with
$e_1\in P_1$ and $e_2\in P_2$, so $q_0$ can lie in neither, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:AS-free}
Theorem~\ref{thm:D} shows that $\mathrm{(FE)}_3$ cannot replace
$\mathrm{(AD)}$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:many}, and that adding $\mathrm{(AS)}$
does not help. The reason $\mathrm{(AS)}$ does not help is visible in the
construction: for antichains it is not a hypothesis at all. If $P$ is any
Borel order of height at most $h$ and $E_0,\dots,E_{h-1}$ is a prescription by
Borel antichains consisting of $\le_P$-minimal points and satisfying
$\mathrm{(FE)}_h$, then attaching to each $e\in\bigcup_iE_i$ a fresh chain
$e<(e,1)<\dots<(e,h-1)$ and declaring $(e,n)$ to lie in the block
$\pi_j(n)$, where $e\in E_j$, produces a Borel order of the same height with
an enlarged prescription satisfying $\mathrm{(FE)}_h$ and $\mathrm{(AS)}$,
from which the original problem is recovered by restriction. The attachment is
harmless precisely because nothing lies below a minimal point, so no
comparabilities between old and new points arise, and the height, being a
supremum over chains each of which is confined to one fibre, does not
increase.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:order-compatible}
Call $\mathrm{(AS)}$ \emph{order-compatible} if the witnessing chain can
always be taken increasing in the index, $x_0<_P\dots<_Px_{h-1}$ with
$x_i\in E_i$. Order-compatible saturation trivialises the problem, for every
$h$ and with no recourse to compactness or invariant separation: let
$c:X\to[h]$ be a Borel map with $x<_Py\Rightarrow c(x)<c(y)$, as furnished by
the Borel Mirsky theorem; along a witnessing chain $c$ is strictly increasing
and takes $h$ values in $[h]$, so $c(x_i)=i$, whence $c(e)=i$ for every
$e\in E_i$, and $A_i=c^{-1}(\{i\})$ is the desired Borel partition. In the
order of Theorem~\ref{thm:D} order-compatibility must therefore fail, and one
sees directly why: it would require two points below $e_2$, which with
$e_2<q_0$ would produce a chain of four. Thus among the two forms of
saturation only the order-compatible one carries information, and the three
patterns realised in Figure~\ref{fig:saturation} are exactly what obstructs
it.
\end{remark}
